\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

In this thesis, we have presented the design, development, and testing of two 
balloon-borne indirect dark matter search experiments, \ac{GAPS} and \ac{GRAMS}. 
My contributions spanned hardware development, data analysis, project management, and scientific simulation, with the shared goal of advancing sensitivity to low-energy cosmic 
antinuclei and indirect dark matter searches.

The \ac{GAPS} experiment successfully launched its science payload on a long-duration balloon flight, demonstrating the viability of its novel detection technique. The data collected from \ac{GAPS} are undergoing analysis, using \ac{GAPS} data I anticipate to provide valuable insights into the nature of cosmic rays and their interactions. We also expect to deliver the first precision measurement of the cosmic antiproton spectrum below 0.25 GeV, see Fig.~\ref{fig:GAPS_antiproton} and the first-ever measurement of the cosmic antideuteron spectrum in $<0.25 GeV/N$ energy range with a sensitivity of $2\times10^{-6}$ [m$^2$ s sr GeV/n]$^{-1}$, see Fig.~\ref{fig:GAPS_antideuteron}.

We also estimated the \ac{aHe3} sensitivity of \ac{GAPS} which is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:cosmic_flux}. With or without candidate events of \ac{aHe3} detected, the \ac{GAPS} data will provide a unique constraint on the cosmic \ac{aHe3} flux in the low energy range, which is crucial for understanding the origin of cosmic rays and their interactions with the interstellar medium.

As for \ac{GRAMS}, we have launched \ac{eGRAMS} as the world's first ever \ac{LArTPC} detector in stratosphere. We have first ever conducted an antiproton beam test to demonstrate the antimatter detection capability of the \ac{LArTPC}. We are expecting to have a \ac{pGRAMS} launch in the coming month to verify the functionality of the instrument to have the first particle-tracking capability and compton camera \ac{LArTPC} detector ever launched on a balloon. The prototype flight \ac{pGRAMS}will also provide valuable data for optimizing the design of the full-scale \ac{GRAMS} instrument, which is expected to be launched in the next few years. With its unique capabilities, \ac{GRAMS} has the potential to make significant contributions to our understanding of $ MeV $ gamma-ray, cosmic rays and dark matter, see Fig.~\ref{fig:GRAMS_MeV_science}, Fig.~\ref{fig:cosmic_flux} and Fig.~\ref{fig:GRAMS_antiproton_excess}. On searching \ac{aHe3}, I established simulation framework combining GRASP, \ac{TOF}-based particle identification ($\varepsilon_{TOF}^{^3\overline{He}}=0.965$, $\varepsilon_{TOF}^{\bar{p}}<1.42\times10^{-4}$ at 99\% C.L.), and charged-pion multiplicity cuts suppressing antiproton contamination to below $10^{-5}$.

Scientifically, these results strengthen indirect dark matter searches in the low-energy antinuclei window and open a practical path toward combined antimatter and MeV gamma-ray observations. In the sub-GeV/n regime, where secondary backgrounds are strongly suppressed, the demonstrated \ac{GAPS} and \ac{GRAMS} capabilities improve sensitivity to rare antinuclei channels that are particularly powerful for testing dark matter scenarios. At the same time, the \ac{LArTPC}-based development for \ac{GRAMS} supports a unified instrument concept that connects antimatter signatures with MeV-domain astrophysics, enabling cross-validated constraints on particle dark matter and high-energy processes in the Galaxy.

Together, these results place both experiments in a strong position to probe dark matter annihilation signatures in the nearly background-free sub-GeV/n antinuclei window. For \ac{GAPS}, the first-flight analysis will deliver calibrated antiproton flux products with atmospheric-unfolding corrections, followed by antideuteron and antihelium candidate searches. My primary focus going forward with \ac{GAPS} will be atmospheric-unfolding corrections. For \ac{pGRAMS}, the prototype flight will validate trigger stability, charge/light reconstruction, and in-flight background behavior against bench and beam-test expectations, providing the technical foundation required to advance toward full-scale \ac{GRAMS} science operations.
In summary, the work presented in this thesis represents a significant step forward in the development of novel detection techniques for indirect dark matter searches. The successful demonstration of the \ac{GAPS} and \ac{GRAMS} instruments, along with the promising preliminary results, sets the stage for exciting discoveries in the coming years as we continue to analyze data and refine our understanding of cosmic rays and dark matter.
